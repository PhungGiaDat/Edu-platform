Six months ago, we launched a product that nobody wanted.

Today, I want to share the lessons that failure taught me — because in the fast-paced world of startups, our setbacks are often our greatest teachers.

When we launched Cartwheel, our team was confident. We had spent 14 months building what we believed was a game-changing solution for freelance invoicing. We had beautiful designs, robust features, and a seamless onboarding flow. What we didn't have was customers.

In the first month, we acquired just 87 users. Our conversion rate from trial to paid sat at a humbling 1.2%. The numbers didn't lie — we had built something people didn't need.

Here's what that experience taught me:

1. Validation isn't a checkbox — it's a discipline. We interviewed 15 potential users before building. It should have been 100.

2. Falling in love with the problem, not the solution, isn't just a cliché — it's survival. We fell in love with our product, and it blinded us.

3. Speed matters more than polish. We spent 14 months perfecting features nobody asked for. Our competitor shipped in 4 and iterated their way to product-market fit.

It's not about avoiding failure — it's about failing fast enough to learn while you still have runway.

Today, our pivot is gaining traction: 2,300 users and growing 15% month-over-month. The journey continues, and I'm grateful for every lesson along the way.

To every founder navigating a setback right now: your failure is not your identity. It's your education.
