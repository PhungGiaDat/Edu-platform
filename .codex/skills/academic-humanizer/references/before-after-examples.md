# Before / after: real pairs from the corpus

Every example below is **verbatim** from the 495-pair dataset (LLM rewrite on
the left, the published human paragraph on the right). They are the ground truth
the rules in SKILL.md are abstracted from. Read these when you want to *calibrate
your ear* before humanizing — the target is not "punchy blog voice," it is this
specific dense, direct, first-person academic register.

---

## Example A — copula, em-dash aside, first person, plain verbs

**LLM:**
> At the core of the data generation system architecture **lies** the OpenAI
> server, **responsible for** generating synthetic respondents. The API **acts
> as** the gateway, **receiving** instructions from the code to initiate data
> generation. It prompts the language model**—GPT-4 in this instance—**to
> **respond** to each listed question...

**Human:**
> Finally, the heart of the data generation system architecture **is** the
> OpenAI server, **which does the heavy lifting of** generating synthetic
> respondents. The API serves as the interface with code **to accept**
> instructions for generating the data. The API asks the LLM **(GPT-4 in our
> case)** to **answer** the questions listed...

Transformations: `lies → is` (restore copula); `responsible for → which does the
heavy lifting of` (a little human personality, not sterile); em-dash appositive
`—GPT-4 in this instance—` → parenthetical `(GPT-4 in our case)`; `in this
instance → in our case` (first person); `respond → answer`; `language model →
LLM`.

## Example B — Latinate verbs, -ing nominalization, semicolon+however

**LLM:**
> In-context learning **enhances** a model's **capacity to comprehend and
> execute** tasks **by leveraging** the context contained within the input,
> **eliminating the need for** further training. ... Few-shot learning is
> cost-effective and simple to implement**; however,** the number of examples
> that can be included **is constrained by** the model's maximum context token
> limit.

**Human:**
> In-context learning **improves** a model's **ability to understand and
> perform** tasks **based on** the context provided within the input, **without
> needing** additional training. ... Few-shot learning entails a low cost and is
> easy to implement**.** The number of examples **is limited by** the maximum
> number of tokens the LLM can process in context.

Transformations: `enhances → improves`; `capacity → ability`; `comprehend →
understand`; `execute → perform`; `leveraging → based on`; `eliminating the need
for → without needing`; `constrained by → limited by`; the `; however,` splice
becomes **two plain sentences**.

## Example C — booster nouns, "the extent to which", padding verbs

**LLM:**
> In Study 1, our **primary goal** is to **evaluate the extent to which** data
> produced by an AI–human hybrid **aligns with** data generated solely by
> humans. To achieve this, we examine **various** LLM-hybrid models, each
> **delegating** one or more **components**... Below, we **provide a detailed
> account of** the data generation **procedures employed**...

**Human:**
> In Study 1, our **first objective** is to **assess how well** the data
> generated by an AI–human hybrid **matches** the human-only data. Our approach
> involves testing **different** LLM-hybrid models in which one or more
> **aspects**... are assigned... Next, we **detail** the data generation
> **process**...

Transformations: `primary goal → first objective`; `evaluate the extent to which
→ assess how well`; `aligns with → matches`; `various → different`; `delegating
→ assigned`; `components → aspects`; `provide a detailed account of → detail`;
`procedures employed → process`; `Below → Next`.

## Example D — "presents our findings", "outlined", signpost openers

**LLM:**
> This section **presents our findings from** evaluating the data produced by
> the four LLM–human hybrid models detailed in Table 5, using the **previously
> outlined** evaluation criteria. We **begin by comparing**... **Following
> this,** we **assess** the LLM-generated data **in relation to** the research
> objectives...

**Human:**
> For the data generated for the four LLM–human hybrids described in Table 5, we
> **next report our findings for** the evaluation criteria **described
> previously**. We **first evaluate**... We **then evaluate** LLM-generated data
> **according to** research objectives...

Transformations: drop the `This section presents...` frame; `presents our
findings from → report our findings for`; `previously outlined → described
previously`; `begin by comparing → first evaluate`; `Following this → then`;
`assess → evaluate`; `in relation to → according to`.

## Example E — em-dash "such as" list, synonym inflation

**LLM:**
> LLMs contribute by **refining** discussion guides, **selecting appropriate**
> interview samples, generating synthetic participants, and analyzing data**—such
> as eliminating redundant** text, **highlighting key** sentences, and
> **organizing them into thematic clusters**.

**Human:**
> LLMs can assist in **streamlining** the discussion guide, **identifying the
> right** sample to interview, creating synthetic interview participants, and
> data analysis **(e.g., removing repetitive** text, **identifying important**
> sentences, and **clustering them thematically)**.

Transformations: em-dash `—such as ...` becomes parenthetical `(e.g., ...)`;
`eliminating → removing`; `redundant → repetitive`; `highlighting → identifying`;
`key → important`; `organizing them into thematic clusters → clustering them
thematically`.

## Example F — cut the inflated appositive, deflate boosters

**LLM:**
> Traditionally, marketing research on consumer responses to innovation has
> **focused primarily on** the point of adoption... **Yet, to fully achieve** BW
> objectives, **it is essential to explore the dynamics that unfold after**
> adoption, **considering both the product perspective and the consumer
> perspective—two interconnected dimensions critical to long-term success.**

**Human:**
> To date, marketing research on consumer response to innovation **typically
> stops at** adoption... **However, to achieve** BW objectives, **it is critical
> to understand what happens after** adoption **from the two interrelated
> perspectives of the product and the consumer.**

Transformations: `focused primarily on the point of → typically stops at`; `Yet
→ However`; drop `fully`; `essential → critical`; `explore the dynamics that
unfold after → understand what happens after`; and **delete the whole trailing
em-dash flourish** "—two interconnected dimensions critical to long-term
success" (pure LLM padding that adds no information).

## What these share

1. The human is **shorter on words but not on content** — it cuts ceremony, not
   substance. Nothing in the human versions is missing; only the padding is gone.
2. **Plain Anglo-Saxon verbs** beat Latinate ones every time: use, show, find,
   answer, improve, match, detail, report, understand.
3. **First person present** carries the claims: *we find, we evaluate, our
   approach, in our case*.
4. **Em dashes become parentheses, commas, or full stops** — without exception
   in these pairs.
5. The human keeps it as **one dense paragraph**; the LLM wants to add headers
   and split.
